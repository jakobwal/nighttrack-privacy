Nighttrack — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 August 2026

Nighttrack keeps everything on your phone. There is no account, no server, and no analytics. The app has no permission to use the internet at all, so nothing it records can be sent anywhere — not to us, not to anyone else.

The only ways anything leaves your phone are the two you start yourself: exporting a backup file, and sharing a night's summary image.

Who this is from

Nighttrack is made by Jakob Wallander, an individual developer in Sweden. Contact: jakobwal@gmail.com.

What the app stores on your phone

All of this lives in Nighttrack's private storage area on your device, which other apps cannot read:

  • Your drinks — what you logged, how much, how strong, and when.
  • Your nights — when a night started and ended, and the summary figures it produced (peak estimate, estimated sober time, totals, spend).
  • Your profile — weight, sex, height and age. These are what the blood-alcohol estimate is calculated from; the app cannot produce a figure without them.
  • Your settings — including whether you have accepted the disclaimer and confirmed you are 18 or over.

That is the whole list. The app does not record your location, your contacts, your photos, your device identifiers, or anything about how you use the app.

What the app does not do

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to.
  • No server. Nighttrack does not hold the INTERNET permission. This is not a promise about our intentions — it is a restriction Android enforces on the app, and you can verify it in the app's own permission list.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier.
  • No ads.
  • No crash reporting. If the app crashes, we do not find out.
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone, ever. There is no data to sell.

When something does leave your phone — and only then

Two features move data off the device, and both require you to act:

  • Export a backup (Settings → Data). This writes a JSON file containing your drinks, nights, profile and settings to a location you choose with Android's own file picker. Once that file exists it is an ordinary file on your device or in whatever storage you picked, and it is yours to look after. If you put it somewhere that syncs to a cloud service, it goes there. The app warns you about this when you export.
  • Share a night's image (History → a night → Share). This renders a picture of that night's summary and hands it to Android's share sheet, where you choose who receives it. The image carries the night's peak estimate and totals along with the disclaimer; it does not contain your profile, your full drink log, or anything about any other night.

Neither of these sends anything to us. We never receive either artefact.

Android's own backup

Android can back apps up to your Google Drive and copy them to a new phone. Nighttrack excludes its database and its settings from both — your drinking history does not silently end up in Google Drive, and it does not travel in a device-to-device transfer. The manual export above is the only sanctioned way to move your data.

Deleting your data

  • Settings → Data → Delete all data removes everything: drinks, nights, summaries, custom drinks, your profile, your settings, and your acceptance of the disclaimer. The app returns to the state it was in when you installed it. There is no undo, and there is no copy of any of it anywhere else.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes the same data, because all of it lives in the app's private storage.

Because nothing is ever sent anywhere, there is no other party to ask, and no request for you to make. The deletion is complete when you make it.

Permissions the app asks for, and why

  • Notifications — so the app can tell you when the estimate has reached 0.0 ‰, when it has stopped rising, and when a finished night has been summed up. The app works without this permission; you just are not told.
  • Alarms & reminders (exact alarms) — optional. Without it, Android may deliver those notifications up to about an hour late.
  • Run at startup — so that a pending notification is re-armed after your phone reboots. Nothing is sent or recorded when this happens; the app simply recalculates the times from the data already on your device.

Age

Nighttrack is for adults. The app asks you to confirm you are 18 or over before it will do anything, and it is not directed at children. If you are under 18, do not use it.

Your rights

The data this app holds is on your device, in your possession, and is never transmitted to us or to anyone else. There is no processing by us to object to, no profile held about you elsewhere, and no third party to restrict. Access, portability and erasure are all available directly in the app: Settings → Data → Export a backup and Delete all data.

If you have a question about any of this, write to jakobwal@gmail.com.

What this policy does not cover

Nighttrack shows estimates, not measurements, and this policy is about your privacy rather than about those figures. What the app can and cannot tell you is set out in full in the app itself, under Settings → About, and you are asked to read and accept it before first use.

Changes

If a future version of Nighttrack collects or transmits anything, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here rather than made quietly. The date at the top says when it was last changed.
